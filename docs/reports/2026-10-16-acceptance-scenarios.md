# Mandatory scenarios (owner's directive, Phase 11) — expected behaviour, tests, result

For each scenario the directive lists: what the system must do, the automated tests that show it, and what is still missing.

- **Result:** every test named here passes in `make check` (see `IMPLEMENTATION-STATUS.md` S17 for the run and its count).
- **Basis:**
  - unless stated otherwise, the tests run the real code against PostgreSQL;
  - the trading tests use the paper simulator with injected faults, or the MT5 emulator driving the real bridge program.
- **Outside pytest:** a real process kill and restart is exercised by `scripts/local-verify.sh` (`--phase chaos` and `restart`).
- Test files are under `tests/`.

## What this run changed

- **Four defects were fixed:**
  - A malformed Telegram callback raised an unhandled error. It is now refused as "Unknown action."
  - The job-history read at worker start would have crashed a worker started during a database outage. It is now best-effort.
  - An approved trade that could not reach the venue failed without telling the owner. It is now reported with the reason, and so is every rejection.
  - An HTTP 200 with a body that is not JSON, in answer to a Binance order request, was read as "not accepted". The order was then marked never sent, although Binance may have accepted it. It is now "outcome unknown": reconciled by client order ID, never resent.
- **A protection gap was closed:** a protective order ended at the venue without our asking (by hand in the venue's app, by the venue, or by someone with access) was replaced silently. Now it also raises a WARNING incident that asks the owner to check the account's access.

## What the run of 2026-10-17 changed

- The three remaining test gaps (scenarios 2, 4 and 17) are closed.
- **The first fixes:**
  - An entry being sent when the emergency stop was pressed stayed open at the venue once acknowledged, and could fill during the stop. The stop cancelled only entries already acknowledged. Now every execution cycle during the stop cancels any entry open at a venue, and the stop's alert counts the entries in flight (ADR-012 addendum, doc 08 §9).
  - A cancel request that never reached the venue was never sent again: the order stayed open there while our record said CANCEL_PENDING. This applied to every cancel: the emergency stop's, the operator's, and the plan's time limit.
- **A review of those fixes** (several independent reviewers, with experiments; the cancel findings also checked by a second reviewer) found more, all now fixed and each covered by a test that fails without its fix:
  - An error in an earlier step of the execution cycle (a corrupt policy, one account's missing adapter) stopped the stop's cancels. Each step, synced order, exit and position now runs on its own; the first error is still raised, and a step failing three times in a row raises its own incident.
  - The first version stopped resending after five sends. A venue outage of about four minutes would have used them all, and nothing would have sent the cancel again. Now the cancel is sent every 5 minutes after the fifth, for as long as the order is cancel pending; the owner is told after the fifth.
  - An alert could open for an order that had just been settled, and never close. The alert now re-checks the order under its lock.
  - An order the venue no longer has (the Binance test network deletes every order when it resets) stayed open in our books forever, and reconciliation counted it as a match. A wiped stop was believed to protect the position. Now such an order is ended after a minute of "no such order", with its own incident, and a stop is replaced.
  - A venue answer read just before a concurrent cancel was applied after it, raising a false CRITICAL "illegal transition". It is now dropped.
  - An HFM cancel with the bridge offline held the execution cycle for 25 s. It now fails at once.
  - The stop's alert promised to cancel in-flight entries even under a policy that keeps them. It now says they stay open.
  - The Telegram bot still asked for, and used up, an authenticator code for a trade decided meanwhile. It now says the trade is decided and leaves the code unused.
- **A second review** (two of its four reviewers finished: the execution logic, and its interactions with the rest of the system) found four more, now fixed with tests that fail without them:
  - On HFM, re-sending an older stop's cancel cleared the stop-loss that a newer stop of the same entry held (MT5 keeps one stop-loss per position).
  - Job health could miss a step failing on every cycle. The cycle now raises `trading.cycle_step_failing:<step>` itself.
  - For an account whose adapter the worker lacked, a cancel was recorded as requested though nothing could be sent.
  - An order settled while reconciliation ran could be reported as a false mismatch.
- **A third review** (the deploy script, the tests and the docs) found no new trading defect. It found deploy-script robustness gaps, four tests that did not check exactly what they claimed, and doc statements that over-claimed; all are corrected. Finally, 21 mutations of the fixes were switched on one at a time on the final code, and each made its test fail. A last review found one older HFM defect (a stop-out after a partial fill was booked wrongly, and detected as an overfill); it was fixed on 2026-10-18 with one stop order per HFM entry.

## Not claimed

Passing these tests does not make the system production-ready for real money. Live trading stays disabled until go-live gates A–I and a separate owner activation. Several scenarios are proven on the paper simulator or the MT5 emulator only. On the real venues, only these are verified so far:
- the HFM demo contract (with an attached stop on a resting order);
- the Binance test network contract.

No real test-network or demo fill has happened yet.
