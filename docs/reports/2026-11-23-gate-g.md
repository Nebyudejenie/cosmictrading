# Gate G for the HFM gold path: the current requirement, why it names Binance, and a proposal (2026-11-23, S115)

Nothing here changes the gate. The code (`trading/golive.py`, `trading/real_book.py`) is unchanged, and a change needs the owner's explicit approval (§6).

## 1. The current requirement

The master build prompt, §1830, Gate G (paper/shadow period):
> "Require an owner-configurable paper/shadow period with a minimum number of tested scenarios and complete audit history. Compare simulated fills with realistic spread, fees, latency, liquidity, and partial-fill assumptions. Paper trading is not proof of live profitability."

The code (S34, S41) passes gate G only when both of these hold:
1. **The paper period:** at least 20 closed simulated trades (`post_trade_reviews.is_simulated`), over at least 30 days counted from the first.
2. **The fill comparison:** at least 20 paper fills compared with Binance's real public order book at the moment of the fill.
   - On median, the paper fill is neither kinder than the real book by more than 5 bps nor harsher by more than 25 bps.
   - None is on a book too thin to fill it, and at most one in five is missed.

**Production, 2026-11-23:**
- 9 of 20 trades: 1 Binance paper trade (2026-10-15) and 8 trades on the HFM demo by the 10,000 demo robot (2026-11-04 to 2026-11-12).
- 38 of 30 days.
- 0 of 20 fill comparisons.

## 2. Why it names Binance

It is not a separate strategy test. It checks the **paper simulator**:
- The simulator fills an order on a stored public quote, up to a minute or two old, plus 5 bps.
- In S41 (2026-10-30), the real-money link being prepared was Binance spot (ADR-034, S40). A paper result is only evidence if the simulator is not kinder than the real venue would have been.
- Binance's order book is public and keyless. The worker reads it right after each paper fill and works out what the same quantity would have cost there.

So the comparison asks whether the simulator flatters the results. It was written for the venue in use then.

## 3. Why it does not fit HFM

The owner's main system is HFM XAUUSD through MT5.

**There are no paper fills on HFM.**
- The robots' HFM trades are filled by HFM's own demo trade server, not by the paper simulator.
- Robot A's simulated account is filled by the gold simulator (`gold/sim.py`), not by the paper broker either.

**Binance's order book says nothing about HFM.** HFM is a market maker on its own quotes, and Binance's PAXG book is a different instrument at a different venue. The paper period's trade count still applies.

**As written, gate G cannot pass on the HFM path.**
- The fill comparison needs paper fills on Binance prices, and the Binance path is paused.
- The trade count only grows while a robot closes demo trades.
- Robot A's 22 closed simulated trades are not counted: they are gold-simulator positions, not post-trade reviews.

## 4. What HFM already provides: measured, not assumed

`measured.fill_slips` (S39) compares each HFM demo fill with HFM's own price at the moment the order was sent: the ask for a buy, the bid for a sell, the stop price for a stop-loss.

These are HFM's own demo executions, labelled SIMULATED because a demo account is not real money.

## 5. The proposal, beside the Binance comparison and never instead of it

For the HFM path, gate G passes only when all of these hold:
1. **The paper period, unchanged:** 20 closed simulated trades over 30 days.
2. **HFM demo fills against HFM's price at sending:**
   - at least 20 market-price fills, median within ±5 bps, none worse than 25 bps;
   - at least 5 stop-loss fills against their stop prices, median within 25 bps.
3. **The simulator against HFM:** at least 20 of Robot A's simulated fills compared with HFM's demo fills of the same decisions (the same rule runs on both). On median, the simulator must not be kinder than HFM's demo by more than 5 bps. This is the exact HFM analogue of "simulated fills against the real venue".
4. **The first live trades are compared the same way**, inside the activation's money cap, and live trading stops by itself if their median slippage passes the limit.

The Binance comparison stays as it is for any Binance live path.

## 6. The risks of this change

- **A demo fills better than live:** no real liquidity, no requotes, perhaps a tighter spread. A comparison on the demo can pass where live fails. Item 4 and the activation's small money cap are the answer; demo figures are never presented as live.
- **It is the same broker's numbers:** HFM's fill against HFM's own quote, not an outside reference like Binance's book. It measures execution, not whether HFM's prices are fair; the cross-check against other gold sources (R10) stays.
- **Fast markets:** news spikes are rare in 20 fills. The stop-loss count (only 1 today) is the weak point, hence the 5 stop fills.
- **Changing a gate can weaken it:** that is why it is added beside the existing comparison, written as code with tests, and named in the gate's text. It is never a silent edit.

## 7. The exact approval needed

> "I approve adding an HFM fill comparison to gate G for the HFM path, beside the Binance comparison: at least 20 HFM demo fills at the market against HFM's price at sending (median within ±5 bps, none worse than 25 bps), at least 5 stop-loss fills against their stop prices, and at least 20 of Robot A's simulated fills against HFM's demo fills of the same decisions (the simulator no kinder than 5 bps on median). The Binance comparison stays for Binance."

Without that approval, gate G stays as it is: NOT MET on the HFM path.
